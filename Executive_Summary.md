# Executive Summary
## Customer Engagement & Product Utilization Analytics for Retention Strategy

**Prepared for:** Government and regulatory stakeholders (e.g., financial oversight bodies)
**Subject:** Behavioral drivers of retail banking customer churn

---

### The Core Finding

Across 10,000 retail banking customers, **engagement behavior — not wealth or demographics — is the dominant driver of customer attrition**. Customers who are financially strong but behaviorally disengaged churn at the highest rate of any group observed (30.5%), more than three times the rate of actively engaged customers (9.7%).

### Why This Matters for Policy and Oversight

- **Consumer protection:** High-balance, inactive customers represent a population at elevated risk of losing banking relationships without warning — relevant to financial inclusion and consumer-protection monitoring.
- **Systemic stability:** Attrition concentrated among premium customers can affect the funding base and stability metrics banks report to regulators.
- **Product design oversight:** Customers holding 3 or more products churn at 80–100%, a pattern consistent with over-selling practices that regulators may wish to monitor under conduct-risk frameworks.

### Headline Numbers

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Overall churn rate | 20.4% |
| Churn rate, active customers | ~13% |
| Churn rate, inactive customers | ~24.5% |
| Churn rate, disengaged premium customers | 30.5% |
| Share of base that is premium yet disengaged | 12.5% |

### Recommendation to Stakeholders

Financial institutions should be encouraged (or required, where applicable) to monitor **engagement-based early-warning indicators** — not only balance and salary thresholds — when assessing customer retention risk and reporting on relationship stability with their customer base.
